The Blood Crisis

Every two seconds, someone
in India needs blood.

India runs short of blood every single day — and the shortage falls hardest on rural families. Commercial blood banks rarely reach them, and the voluntary networks that quietly save lives in cities simply don't exist where they live.

~3M
Units of blood short annually in India against the country's total requirement — a gap concentrated in rural and semi-urban areas.
<1%
Of India's eligible population donates blood voluntarily. The vast majority of rural patients depend on replacement donors — family members who are often themselves anaemic or ineligible.
Hours
The average time a rural patient's family spends searching for blood in an emergency — time that is often the difference between life and death in trauma and obstetric cases.

The mother who haemorrhages during childbirth. The accident victim on a rural road. The child with thalassemia needing regular transfusions. All of them are waiting — for a donor, for a match, for a system that doesn't yet exist at scale. Raktify is building that system.

How It Works

A network that activates
in minutes, not days.

From the moment a need is raised to the moment a patient is safe — here is how Raktify turns a network of willing strangers into help that arrives in time.

1
Need Identified
A hospital, PHC, or family member flags an urgent blood requirement through the Raktify network — specifying blood group, quantity, and location.
2
Network Activated
Registered voluntary donors in the relevant blood group and geographic area are immediately notified. Our coordinator network reaches donors via WhatsApp, SMS, and direct call.
3
Donor Confirmed & Connected
A willing donor is matched and connected to the patient's family or hospital directly. Raktify coordinates logistics support — transport guidance, documentation, and accompaniment if needed.
4
Donation Completed
The donor gives blood at the hospital's blood bank. The patient receives verified, screened blood with no commercial transaction. The donor's record is updated and they are honoured by the network.
5
Follow-up & Network Growth
Every successful donation strengthens the network. Donors are thanked, their records maintained, and the resolved case shared (with consent) to inspire more registrations in the community.

Who We Serve
🤱 Mothers — Post-partum haemorrhage remains a leading cause of maternal mortality in rural India.
🚑 Trauma patients — Road accidents in rural areas where the nearest blood bank may be hours away.
🧒 Children — Thalassemia, sickle cell anaemia, and other conditions requiring regular transfusions.
🏥 Surgery patients — Elective and emergency surgeries in district hospitals without reliable blood supply.
💉 Cancer patients — Ongoing transfusion needs that commercial banks cannot reliably support.
Our Approach

Three pillars of
a national blood network.

Raktify isn't a single app or a one-city drive. It's a three-layer system built to hold up under pressure and scale across India — village by village, hospital by hospital.

👥
Community Donor Registry
A continuously growing database of verified voluntary donors — segmented by blood group, geography, and last donation date. Donors opt in, stay informed, and are activated only when eligible and available.
📱
Real-time Coordination
Multi-channel notification and coordination — WhatsApp groups, SMS broadcasts, coordinator call trees, and a digital request-tracking system that ensures no need goes unresponded to.
🏥
Hospital & PHC Integration
Formal partnerships with district hospitals, CHCs, and PHCs ensure that when a blood need arises, Raktify is already in the system. Nurses and ASHA workers know who to call.
