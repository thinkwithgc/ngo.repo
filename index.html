Our Mission

Born from a gap that
metro India never sees.

"We built Choudhari EduHealth because the healthcare gap in rural India is invisible to those who live in cities — and that invisibility is the problem."

Established in May 2025, Choudhari EduHealth India Foundation was founded by two professionals rooted in technology and management who grew up witnessing the stark difference in healthcare access between urban and rural India.

Hundreds of millions of Indians live in villages where a qualified doctor visits once a month — if at all. Emergency blood needs go unmet because voluntary donor networks don't exist at scale. We are building the infrastructure to change that: starting on the ground, with measurable outcomes, and expanding across the country.

🏥
Dr. GharoGhari — Mobile OPD
A dual-shift mobile clinic van delivering free consultations, diagnostics and medicines to underserved villages — starting its pilot in rural India and scaling district by district.
🩸
BloodConnect — National Donor Network
A nationwide voluntary blood donor coordination network connecting willing donors to patients in critical need — eliminating dangerous delays caused by the absence of organised blood access in rural areas.
📚
EduHealth Awareness
Community health literacy campaigns, school health checkups, and preventive care sessions delivered alongside our mobile OPD operations.

Launching — Phase 1 Pilot
Mobile Healthcare · Rural India (Expanding)
Dr. GharoGhari
"The doctor who comes home"
A fully-equipped mobile OPD van operating dual shifts — morning and evening — to match the real availability patterns of rural daily-wage workers and farming communities. Every working day, the van covers two villages, delivering free consultations, point-of-care diagnostics, and dispensed medicines to populations far from district hospitals. The model is built to replicate: one pilot van establishes the operational playbook, and each additional van extends coverage to a new district.

Blood Access · National Programme
BloodConnect
"No patient should wait for blood."
A technology-enabled voluntary donor coordination network designed to eliminate emergency blood shortages across India. BloodConnect connects willing voluntary donors to patients in critical need — building a live, community-powered blood access layer that reduces dangerous dependency on commercial blood banks and cuts life-threatening delays in areas where organised blood storage infrastructure is absent or unreliable. The programme is designed for national scale from day one, with simultaneous rollout across multiple locations.
